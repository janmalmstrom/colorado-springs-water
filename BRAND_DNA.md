## 3. Core Problem We Solve

Water damage gets worse every hour it is not treated. Within 24 hours: drywall absorbs water and swells. Within 48–72 hours: mold begins growing. Within a week: structural compromise begins in framing and subfloor. The faster a professional extracts water and begins structural drying, the lower the final repair bill — and the lower the risk of a mold remediation claim on top of the water damage claim.

Colorado Springs has unique challenges: hard freeze winters (burst pipes), rapid snowmelt cycles in spring, and severe afternoon thunderstorms (basement flooding). El Paso County has one of the higher rates of water damage insurance claims in Colorado.

## 4. Positioning

**One sentence:** Colorado Springs's 24/7 water damage restoration — IICRC certified, insurance approved, same-day response.

**vs. DIY:** Consumer water extractors move a fraction of the water that commercial truck-mounted extraction units do. Structural drying requires industrial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated with moisture meters — not box fans. Incomplete drying leads to mold, which turns a water claim into a much larger mold remediation claim.

**vs. general contractors:** General contractors rebuild after water damage is resolved. Water damage restoration is a distinct phase: extraction → drying → documentation → rebuild. Doing it in the wrong order, or without moisture monitoring, causes callbacks and mold.

**vs. out-of-town storm chasers:** After major Colorado storms, out-of-area contractors arrive and disappear. A local company has an established reputation, insurance relationships, and will be here for follow-up if issues arise.

## 9. Content Rules — ACCURACY IS MANDATORY

**Water categories (IICRC S500 standard):**
- **Category 1** (clean water): from supply lines, drinking water sources. Lowest contamination risk.
- **Category 2** (gray water): from dishwashers, washing machines, toilet tank overflow (not bowl). Contaminated, requires antimicrobial treatment.
- **Category 3** (black water): sewage, floodwater from outside, toilet bowl overflow. Highest contamination — Category 3 material cannot be dried and must be removed.

**Mold timing (IICRC standard):**
- Mold can begin growing in 24–48 hours on wet organic materials at room temperature
- This is not a scare tactic — it is the standard cited in the IICRC S520 standard
- Do not say "mold will definitely grow" — say "mold can begin growing"

**IICRC certification:**
- IICRC = Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification
- Industry-standard certification for water damage, fire damage, and mold remediation
- Not a government license — a voluntary industry certification that sets technical standards
- A contractor can be IICRC certified; a contractor can also have IICRC-certified technicians

**Insurance claims:**
- Most homeowners insurance covers sudden and accidental water damage (burst pipe, appliance failure)
- Flood insurance is separate (NFIP or private) and typically required for storm floodwater
- Gradual damage (slow leak over time) is often excluded — insurers call this a maintenance issue
- We document damage for the adjuster; we do not determine coverage

**Colorado-specific:**
- Colorado Springs sits at ~6,000 ft elevation, with freeze risk from October–April
- Freeze-thaw cycles cause pipe failures that general contractors in warmer climates don't see
- El Paso County, Teller County, Pueblo County service area

## 13. Local Intelligence

### Neighborhoods & Areas
- **Briargate** (north, newer construction, 2000s-2010s homes): basement finishing is common, sump pump failures → high water damage frequency
- **Black Forest** (northeast, heavily wooded): wildfire area, but also freeze damage from exposed pipes in rural lots
- **Broadmoor / Southwest** (higher income, older homes): clients expect premium service, documentation for high-value finishes
- **Fountain Valley / Fountain** (south, military families): transient population, renters don't know property history → undiscovered previous damage common
- **Old Colorado City** (historic district, west): older plumbing (galvanized/lead), no backflow prevention → sewage backup risk
- **Security-Widefield** (southeast, working class): price-sensitive, DIY attempts common before calling professional

### Local Price Benchmarks (Colorado Springs market, 2024–2025)
- **Water extraction only (small area, <500 sq ft):** $500–$1,200
- **Full water damage restoration (average residential):** $3,500–$8,000
- **Category 3 / sewage backup cleanup:** $7,000–$15,000
- **Mold remediation (small area):** $1,500–$4,000
- **Mold remediation (large/structural):** $8,000–$20,000
- **Insurance deductible typical range:** $1,000–$2,500 (check policy)
- **Competitor reference:** ServiceMaster, Servpro charge national rates; local independents often 15–20% lower

### Regional Specifics
- **Altitude (6,035 ft):** freeze risk Oct–April; pipes in exterior walls, garages, and crawl spaces freeze faster than at sea level
- **Hailstorms:** CO Springs averages 5+ hail events/year → roof damage → attic water intrusion — peak season May–August
- **Wildfire smoke/ash:** post-wildfire water runoff can introduce contamination not covered by standard Category 1 assumptions (relevant for Waldo Canyon / Black Forest wildfire areas)
- **Military installations (Fort Carson, Peterson SFB, Schriever SFB, NORAD/Cheyenne Mountain):** large renter market, property managers handle claims → establish B2B relationships
- **HOA coverage gaps:** many Springs HOAs have master policies that stop at the unit walls — residents often don't realize they're responsible for interior water damage
- **El Paso County building permits:** required for structural repairs post-water damage (drywall replacement, flooring replacement over certain sq footage) — most homeowners don't know this

### Coined Terms
- **"Altitude Freeze" damage** — pipe failures caused by Colorado's high-elevation freeze-thaw cycle (vs standard burst pipe language)
- **"Hail-to-Water path"** — roof hail puncture → attic water intrusion → ceiling damage sequence (local seasonal pattern)
- **"Military property gap"** — coverage confusion for Fort Carson / Peterson area renters who assume landlord's policy covers interior water damage
