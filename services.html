First Response

Water Extraction

Extraction is the first and most critical step in water damage restoration. The goal is to remove as much water as possible before structural drying begins — the more water extracted, the shorter the drying time and the less material needs to be replaced.

We use truck-mounted extraction units capable of removing thousands of gallons per hour. These are not portable shop vacuums — commercial truck-mounted equipment creates significantly stronger suction and can extract water from carpet, pad, and surface materials that consumer equipment cannot reach. For hardwood floors and subfloor materials, we may use specialized floor mat extraction systems.

After extraction, we use thermal imaging cameras to locate hidden moisture in walls, under flooring, and in ceiling cavities that is not visible to the naked eye. Water migrates further than it appears — locating all affected areas before drying begins prevents missed moisture that leads to mold later.

What We Use

  • Truck-mounted extraction units
  • Portable extractors for difficult access areas
  • Hardwood floor mat extraction systems
  • Thermal imaging cameras
  • Professional moisture meters

What Gets Extracted

  • Standing water
  • Water in carpet and pad
  • Surface water on hard floors
  • Water in subfloor materials
  • Water in wall cavities (via access points)

What Happens Next

  • Thermal imaging sweep for hidden moisture
  • Decision: what can be dried vs what must be removed
  • Setup of structural drying equipment
  • Moisture readings documented for insurance
IICRC S500 Standard

Structural Drying

After extraction, the structure must be dried to IICRC S500 standard moisture levels before any rebuild work begins. Drying is a science, not a matter of opening windows or running a few dehumidifiers. Industrial dehumidifiers are placed based on psychrometric calculations — the relationship between temperature, humidity, and airflow that determines how fast materials will dry.

High-velocity air movers are positioned to create airflow patterns that move moisture from building materials into the air, where the dehumidifiers capture it. We take moisture readings every 24 hours at documented measurement points and log the progress. This daily moisture log is critical evidence for your insurance claim — it documents that drying was performed properly and completed to acceptable levels.

Drying typically takes 3–5 days for Category 1 losses, longer for more extensive damage or Category 2/3 situations. The drying timeline depends on material types, extent of saturation, and ambient conditions. We do not stop drying early to save time — incomplete drying is the primary cause of mold after water damage restoration.

Equipment Used

  • LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers
  • High-velocity air movers
  • Desiccant dehumidifiers (severe cases)
  • Injectidry wall cavity drying systems
  • Floor drying mats

Monitoring & Documentation

  • Daily moisture readings at mapped points
  • Psychrometric data logged daily
  • Progress photos for insurance
  • Drying complete when materials reach target MC
  • Drying report provided at completion

Typical Timeline

  • Category 1 (clean water): 3–5 days
  • Category 2 (gray water): 4–7 days
  • Category 3 (black water): requires demolition first
  • Hardwood floors: 7–14 days (if salvageable)
  • Crawlspace: 5–10 days
Category 3 — Black Water Protocol

Sewage Cleanup & Contaminated Water Remediation

Sewage backup, toilet overflow, and floodwater from outside the structure are classified as Category 3 water (black water) under the IICRC S500 standard. Category 3 water contains pathogens including bacteria, viruses, and parasites. It cannot be dried in place — contaminated materials must be removed, regardless of whether they appear structurally sound.

This is the most important distinction in water damage restoration: porous materials (drywall, carpet, pad, insulation) that contact Category 3 water must be removed. Drying them in place does not remove the contamination. Antimicrobial treatments reduce surface contamination but do not address pathogens embedded in porous materials.

Our Category 3 protocol: containment of the affected area, removal of contaminated materials, application of EPA-registered antimicrobial disinfectants to structural surfaces, HEPA air scrubbing, and drying. We provide documentation of the Category 3 designation and scope of demolition for your insurance adjuster.

Category 3 Sources

  • Sewage backup through drains or toilets
  • Floodwater from outside the structure
  • Groundwater intrusion through foundation
  • Category 1 or 2 water left untreated for 72+ hours
  • Water from rivers, streams, or retention ponds

Materials That Must Be Removed

  • Drywall that contacted Category 3 water
  • Carpet and pad
  • Insulation
  • Flooring with porous backing
  • Any organic material that cannot be disinfected

Our Protocol

  • Containment and PPE
  • Removal of Category 3 materials
  • EPA-registered disinfectant application
  • HEPA air scrubbing
  • Structural drying of remaining materials
  • Documentation for insurance
IICRC S520 Standard

Mold Remediation

Mold can begin growing on wet organic materials within 24–48 hours under typical indoor conditions. If water damage went undetected, was incompletely dried, or was present in a hidden area (inside a wall, under flooring), mold growth is a common result. Mold remediation is a distinct scope of work from water damage restoration and follows the IICRC S520 standard.

Remediation involves containment of the affected area, removal of mold-contaminated materials, HEPA vacuuming, application of antimicrobial treatments to structural surfaces, and air scrubbing with HEPA filtration. Clearance testing by an independent industrial hygienist can confirm that remediation is complete — we can coordinate this if required by your insurer or for your own peace of mind.

Signs You May Have Mold

  • Musty odor in specific area
  • Past water damage that wasn't professionally dried
  • Visible discoloration on drywall or wood
  • Persistent respiratory symptoms in occupants
  • Water stain that appeared and was "dried out" by fans

Remediation Protocol

  • Assessment and scope development
  • Containment (negative air pressure)
  • Removal of mold-contaminated materials
  • HEPA vacuuming of structural surfaces
  • Antimicrobial treatment
  • Air scrubbing with HEPA filtration

Insurance & Mold

  • Mold resulting from a covered water event may be covered
  • Pre-existing mold is typically excluded
  • Mold from "gradual damage" (slow leak) often excluded
  • We document the mold's origin for your adjuster
  • Independent clearance testing available on request
Claims Navigation

Insurance Claim Assistance

Most homeowners insurance covers sudden and accidental water damage — a burst pipe, appliance failure, or sudden roof leak. Flood damage from outside the structure typically requires a separate flood insurance policy (NFIP or private). Gradual damage from a slow leak over time is often excluded as a maintenance issue.

We work directly with all major insurance carriers including State Farm, USAA, Allstate, Farmers, Nationwide, Liberty Mutual, and others. Our documentation package includes: daily moisture readings, before and after photography, itemized scope of work in Xactimate format (the industry-standard estimating software most adjusters use), material samples if needed, and a drying report upon completion.

We cannot guarantee claim approval or specific payout amounts — coverage is determined by your policy and adjuster. What we can guarantee is that our documentation gives your claim the best possible support.

What We Provide

  • Daily moisture reading logs
  • Before, during, and after photos
  • Xactimate-compatible scope of work
  • Equipment inventory and placement logs
  • Completed drying report
  • Direct communication with your adjuster

Typically Covered

  • Burst or frozen pipe
  • Appliance failure (sudden)
  • Sudden roof leak from storm
  • Accidental overflow (tub, sink)
  • Ice dam water intrusion (varies by policy)

Often Excluded

  • Gradual leaks / maintenance issues
  • Ground flooding (without flood policy)
  • Sewer backup (without endorsement)
  • Mold from pre-existing conditions
  • Negligence / deferred maintenance
