Emergency Response & First Steps

What should I do immediately after discovering water damage?

In order of priority:

  • Stop the water source. Turn off the main water shutoff if a pipe burst. If the source is outside flooding, you cannot stop it — focus on the next steps.
  • Turn off electricity in flooded areas. Water and live circuits are a fatal combination. If you cannot safely reach the breaker panel, stay out of the area.
  • Document before touching anything. Take photos and video of everything — standing water levels, affected rooms, visible damage. This documentation is critical for your insurance claim.
  • Remove valuables from standing water if it is safe to do so.
  • Call your insurance company to open a claim. Most policies require timely notification.
  • Call a water damage restoration company. The sooner professional extraction begins, the less damage occurs.

Do not attempt to use a household vacuum on standing water. It is not designed for this, creates electrical hazard, and removes a fraction of the water that needs to be extracted.

How quickly should I call a water damage company?

As quickly as possible — ideally within the first hour or two. Water spreads and absorbs into building materials continuously. Drywall, wood framing, subfloor OSB, and insulation all absorb water. The longer they stay saturated, the more material must be replaced rather than dried in place.

More critically: the IICRC S520 standard identifies 24–48 hours as the window in which mold can begin growing on wet organic materials at room temperature. Professional extraction and drying begun within the first few hours dramatically reduces the probability of mold development.

Is it safe to be in my house during water damage restoration?

For Category 1 (clean water from supply lines) losses, remaining in the home during drying is generally fine. For Category 2 or 3 losses — gray water (dishwasher, washing machine overflow) or black water (sewage, outside floodwater) — temporary displacement may be necessary during the active remediation phase due to contamination, antimicrobial treatments, and odor.

The restoration equipment is loud. Industrial dehumidifiers and air movers run 24 hours a day during the drying phase. Many homeowners choose to stay elsewhere during this period simply for livability reasons. We advise on a case-by-case basis based on the specific loss category and scope.

Water Extraction & Structural Drying

Can I dry water damage myself with fans and a dehumidifier?

Consumer fans move air surface-level but do not pull moisture out of building materials — they primarily move it around. A consumer dehumidifier processes 30–70 pints per day. Industrial LGR dehumidifiers process 150–250+ pints per day, and multiple units are deployed based on psychrometric calculations for the specific space.

More importantly: professional drying requires moisture meter readings at documented points to confirm drying is complete. Without this, you cannot know whether materials have dried to acceptable moisture content or whether there is trapped moisture that will lead to mold. Insurance adjusters also require this documentation to confirm drying was performed properly.

DIY drying on a minor loss (small area, caught immediately, clean water) is possible in some situations. For anything beyond the most minor spill, the risk of incomplete drying outweighs the cost savings.

How long does water damage drying take?

Typical drying timelines per IICRC S500 standard:

  • Category 1, small area: 3–5 days
  • Category 1, larger loss: 4–7 days
  • Category 2: 4–7 days after material removal
  • Hardwood floors (if salvageable): 7–14 days with specialized drying mats
  • Crawlspace: 5–10 days

These are estimates — actual drying time depends on material types, extent of saturation, starting moisture levels, and ambient conditions. At Colorado Springs' elevation (approximately 6,000 feet), lower humidity often aids drying, but winter conditions with cold ambient temperatures can slow it. We provide daily moisture readings until the structure reaches target moisture content.

Flood Damage Restoration

My basement flooded from heavy rain — what category of water damage is this?

Floodwater that enters from outside the structure — through foundation cracks, window wells, or surface flooding — is classified as Category 3 (black water) under the IICRC S500 standard. This is because exterior floodwater carries soil contaminants, fertilizers, pesticides, and potentially sewage from overwhelmed municipal systems.

Category 3 water cannot be dried in place. Porous materials (drywall, insulation, carpet) that contacted floodwater must be removed regardless of whether they look or smell contaminated. Structural surfaces (concrete block, treated lumber) can be cleaned, disinfected, and dried. This is a harder conversation than Category 1 losses, but it is the correct protocol to prevent contamination-related health issues.

This also has insurance implications: most homeowners policies do not cover ground flooding. Flood insurance (NFIP or private) is required for exterior floodwater coverage. We document the source and category of loss carefully for insurance purposes.

Colorado Springs basement flooding from snowmelt — is this covered by insurance?

Colorado Springs' spring snowmelt creates significant basement flooding risk each year. Whether this is covered by insurance depends on the entry point:

  • Sump pump failure: Covered under most policies if you have sump pump failure coverage (an endorsement, not standard). Without this endorsement, sump pump failures are typically excluded.
  • Water backing up through a floor drain: May be covered under sewer/drain backup endorsement.
  • Seepage through foundation walls or floor cracks: Generally excluded as a maintenance/seepage issue.
  • Surface water from snowmelt flooding: Flood insurance required.

We document the entry point and circumstances carefully, which helps your adjuster determine coverage. We recommend reviewing your policy for sump pump and sewer backup endorsements before spring.

Sewage Cleanup

How dangerous is sewage backup in my home?

Sewage (Category 3 black water) contains bacteria, viruses, and parasites including E. coli, Salmonella, Hepatitis A, and others. Direct contact — particularly with open wounds — or ingestion (contaminated water reaching food preparation surfaces) poses genuine health risks. The area should be avoided by occupants, especially children, elderly individuals, and anyone immunocompromised, until professional remediation is complete.

Odor alone is not the risk indicator — contamination may be present in materials that have been surface-cleaned and appear normal. Professional remediation includes containment, removal of contaminated porous materials, EPA-registered disinfectant application, and HEPA air scrubbing to address airborne particles.

Mold Prevention & Remediation

How quickly can mold grow after water damage?

The IICRC S520 standard identifies 24–48 hours as the window in which mold can begin growing on wet organic materials at normal indoor temperatures (68–77°F / 20–25°C). Mold requires three things: a food source (organic material like drywall paper, wood, or carpet), moisture, and temperature in a habitable range. The first two are present in most water damage situations.

The 24–48 hour figure is not a guarantee — conditions vary, and mold may appear later or not at all if drying is prompt and thorough. But it is the conservative planning threshold that IICRC-trained technicians work from. This is why rapid extraction and drying is prioritized over other considerations.

How do I know if I have mold after water damage?

Visible mold is the clearest indicator — black, green, white, or gray discoloration on drywall, wood, or other surfaces. A musty odor (earthy, damp smell) often precedes visible growth. Other indicators include water stains that appeared without a known recent source, or past water damage that was not professionally dried.

Mold also grows in hidden locations: inside wall cavities, under flooring, in crawlspaces, and above ceiling tiles. A moisture meter assessment and visual inspection can identify areas of concern. If you suspect mold but cannot locate the source, an industrial hygienist can conduct air and surface sampling to confirm its presence and identify the species.

Insurance Claims & Documentation

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage in Colorado?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage. Common covered causes:

  • Burst or frozen pipes
  • Appliance failure (water heater, dishwasher, washing machine)
  • Sudden overflow from tub, toilet, or sink
  • Storm damage causing water intrusion (roof damage)
  • Ice dam damage (varies by policy)

Common exclusions:

  • Ground flooding / surface water (requires separate flood policy)
  • Gradual leaks — a slow drip over weeks or months
  • Sewer/drain backup (requires endorsement in most policies)
  • Sump pump failure (requires endorsement)
  • Neglected maintenance issues

Coverage is ultimately determined by your specific policy and adjuster. We provide documentation that clearly establishes the cause and timeline of loss, which is the most important thing you can do to support a claim.

Should I call my insurance company before calling a water damage company?

Call both as quickly as possible, in whatever order works for you. Most policies require "prompt" notification — delaying the insurance call by days may create a coverage issue. Most water damage companies can begin work before an adjuster visits; the adjuster typically assesses after extraction and during drying.

One important note: you are not required to use your insurance company's preferred contractor list. You have the right to hire any licensed, qualified contractor. Preferred contractor programs benefit insurers primarily. IICRC certified contractors with proper documentation are accepted by all major carriers regardless of preferred status.

Cost & Pricing

How much does water damage restoration cost in Colorado Springs?

Cost varies enormously by scope, category, and size. Rough ranges:

  • Small Category 1 loss (one room, caught quickly): $1,500–$4,000 for extraction and drying
  • Medium Category 1 loss (several rooms, clean water): $4,000–$10,000
  • Category 2 or 3 with material removal: $8,000–$20,000+
  • Large loss with rebuild required: $20,000–$75,000+
  • Mold remediation (additional): $2,000–$15,000 depending on scope

These are rough ranges — actual cost depends on square footage, materials affected, water category, and whether contents need to be packed out. We provide a free on-site estimate with moisture readings before any work begins. There are no surprises.

Choosing a Water Damage Contractor

What is IICRC certification and why does it matter?

IICRC (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) is the industry organization that writes the technical standards for water damage restoration (S500), mold remediation (S520), and other restoration disciplines. IICRC certified technicians have completed structured training and passed exams — they follow established protocols, not improvised approaches.

IICRC certification matters for several reasons: technical quality of work, insurance documentation (insurers recognize IICRC protocols as the industry standard), and accountability (certified professionals can lose their certification for improper practices). When a contractor says their work follows "IICRC standards," they should be able to show their technician certifications — not just claim compliance.

How do I avoid storm chaser contractors after a water or flood event?

After major weather events in Colorado Springs, out-of-area contractors arrive soliciting work. Warning signs:

  • No local address — uses a P.O. box or out-of-state address
  • Cannot provide a Colorado contractor license number
  • Asks you to sign an Assignment of Benefits (AOB) form upfront — this transfers your insurance claim rights to the contractor
  • No verifiable reviews, no BBB listing, no IICRC certification
  • Unusually high-pressure sales tactics immediately after a loss event
  • Vague or verbal estimates with no written scope of work

A legitimate local contractor will provide a written estimate, verifiable licensing and insurance, and will not pressure you to sign documents before you've had time to review them.
