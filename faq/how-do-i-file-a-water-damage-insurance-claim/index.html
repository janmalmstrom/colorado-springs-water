How do I file a water damage insurance claim?

By Sarah Mitchell
Quick Answer

File your water damage insurance claim immediately by calling your insurer, documenting all damage with photos, and requesting an adjuster inspection before any repairs begin.

Key Takeaways

  • Most homeowners insurance covers sudden and accidental water damage (burst pipes, appliance failures) but excludes gradual leaks and exterior floodwater — which requires a separate flood policy.
  • Colorado's freeze-thaw cycle at 6,000 ft elevation makes burst pipe claims among the most common in El Paso County — document pipe location and freeze conditions as part of your claim.
  • Restoration contractors document daily moisture readings and drywall cavity readings using calibrated meters — this log is required evidence for your adjuster and follows IICRC S500 drying protocols.
  • Sewer backup is excluded from standard homeowners policies in Colorado unless you've added a separate sewer backup endorsement — check your policy declarations page before assuming coverage.

What Does Colorado Homeowners Insurance Actually Cover for Water Damage?

Not all water damage is treated the same way under a standard homeowners policy. The Insurance Information Institute draws a consistent distinction: sudden and accidental water damage is typically covered; gradual damage and exterior flooding are typically not.

  • Covered (usually): Burst pipes from Colorado's freeze-thaw cycle, appliance failures (dishwasher, water heater, washing machine), sudden roof leaks from hail or wind, accidental overflow from a bathtub or sink.
  • Not covered (usually): Gradual leaks you knew or should have known about, exterior floodwater from snowmelt or storm runoff, ground seepage into a basement, sewer backup without a separate endorsement.
  • Requires separate flood policy: Storm-driven flooding from outside the home — including the snowmelt flooding that affects lower-elevation neighborhoods like Fountain and Security-Widefield each spring — is only covered under an NFIP or private flood policy, not a standard homeowners policy.
⚠️ Watch out: Sewer backup is one of the most misunderstood coverage gaps in Colorado Springs. A standard homeowners policy does not cover sewage that backs up through floor drains or toilets unless you've specifically added a sewer backup endorsement. Check your declarations page now — not after the backup happens. The Colorado Division of Insurance maintains a consumer helpline (1-800-930-3745) if you have questions about what your policy requires.

El Paso County sits at roughly 6,035 feet of elevation. The October-through-April freeze risk here is more severe than in warmer climates, making pipes in exterior walls, garages, and unheated crawl spaces particularly vulnerable. When freeze damage causes a burst pipe, that's generally covered — but your adjuster may verify whether heat was maintained in the home. If you left for an extended period and reduced the thermostat below a temperature your policy specifies (some Colorado policies require a minimum indoor temperature of 55°F), that exclusion can apply.

Step-by-Step: How to File a Water Damage Insurance Claim

  1. Stop the water source first. Shut off the main water supply if it's a pipe or appliance failure. Shut off power to affected areas if standing water is present. These are safety steps — not repairs — and don't affect your claim.
  2. Document before you touch anything. Use your phone to photograph and video every affected room: standing water depth, waterlines on walls, damaged flooring, wet insulation, affected contents. Do this before extraction begins. Adjusters require pre-remediation documentation.
  3. Call your insurance company to open a claim. Report the loss as soon as possible. Most policies have prompt reporting requirements — waiting days can create questions about whether damage was pre-existing or expanded due to delay. Get your claim number and assigned adjuster's contact information in writing.
  4. Contact a local water damage restoration contractor immediately. Begin emergency extraction without waiting for adjuster approval. Per IICRC S520 standards, mold can begin growing within 24–48 hours on wet organic materials. Most policies require you to mitigate further damage. The contractor can simultaneously document damage for your adjuster.
  5. Request a detailed moisture log from your restoration contractor. IICRC S500 (Standard and Reference Guide for Professional Water Damage Restoration) requires documented moisture readings throughout the drying process. Your contractor should provide daily psychrometric readings — temperature, relative humidity, moisture content of building materials — using calibrated meters. Your adjuster needs this log to approve the drying scope.
  6. Meet the adjuster with documentation ready. Provide your photo and video evidence, the contractor's written scope of work, and the moisture log. If you have pre-loss photos of the affected area (home inspection photos, renovation photos), include those — they establish the pre-damage condition of flooring and finishes.
  7. Review the adjuster's estimate before signing anything. Verify that the scope includes all affected materials — cavity drying behind walls, subfloor drying, and contents coverage if applicable. If the estimate seems incomplete, request a line-item breakdown.
💡 Tip: El Paso County requires building permits for structural repairs above a certain scope — including full drywall replacement and flooring replacement in certain configurations. A licensed local contractor will pull the required permits, which creates documentation for your insurer. Out-of-area contractors sometimes skip permits to move fast — a choice that can create compliance problems for you later.

What Documentation Do Adjusters Actually Need?

An insurance adjuster's job is to verify what happened, confirm it's a covered cause of loss, and establish the scope and cost of repair. Restoration contractors trained in IICRC standards produce documentation that aligns directly with adjuster requirements under Colorado homeowners policies.

Documentation Checklist for Your Water Damage Claim

  • Photos and video of standing water, waterlines, and all affected rooms — taken before extraction begins
  • Date and time of discovery (this matters for prompt-reporting requirements)
  • Source of water identified (burst pipe location, appliance involved, toilet overflow, etc.)
  • Weather records if applicable (hail report, freeze temperature data) — local weather data for El Paso County is available through NOAA and can corroborate a freeze claim
  • Written scope of work from the restoration contractor with IICRC water category classification (Category 1, 2, or 3)
  • Daily moisture logs with psychrometric readings (per IICRC S500 protocol)
  • Contents inventory of damaged personal property with estimated replacement values
  • Any prior repair records showing the property was properly maintained (helps counter gradual damage exclusion arguments)
  • Permits pulled for structural repairs, once restoration begins

The IICRC water category assigned by the restoration contractor directly affects drying protocol and cost structure. Category 1 (clean water from a supply line) has a different drying protocol than Category 2 (gray water from a dishwasher or washing machine) or Category 3 (black water from sewage or exterior floodwater). A contractor who uses IICRC-standard classifications makes the adjuster's review faster and reduces scope disputes.

What If the Adjuster Disputes the Scope or Underpays?

Adjusters work for the insurance company, and initial estimates sometimes omit secondary damage — mold prevention treatments, cavity drying behind walls, subfloor moisture content — that isn't visible during a brief walkthrough. You have recourse.

  • Request a re-inspection with the contractor present. Having the restoration contractor walk the scope with the adjuster directly resolves most disputes about what materials were affected.
  • File a complaint with the Colorado Division of Insurance. If you believe your claim is being improperly denied or delayed, the Colorado Division of Insurance accepts consumer complaints and can review insurer conduct under Colorado insurance regulations.
  • Hire a public adjuster. Public adjusters work for the policyholder (not the insurer) and are licensed by the Colorado Division of Insurance. They take a percentage of the claim settlement but typically recover more than enough to cover their fee on disputed claims. This is worth considering on losses above $10,000.
  • Invoke the appraisal clause. Most Colorado homeowners policies include an appraisal clause — a binding dispute resolution process where each party selects an independent appraiser, and a neutral umpire resolves disagreements. This is faster and less expensive than litigation.
💡 Tip: Fort Carson, Peterson Space Force Base, and Schriever SFB bring a large population of military renters to Colorado Springs. If you're a renter, your landlord's policy covers the building structure — not your personal property and not your additional living expenses if you're displaced. Renters insurance with water damage coverage and temporary housing provisions is a separate policy. Many military renters in the Fountain Valley and Security-Widefield areas discover this gap only after a water event.

How Long Does a Water Damage Claim Take to Resolve in Colorado?

Colorado insurance regulations require insurers to acknowledge a claim within 10 business days and to accept or deny a claim within 60 days of receiving proof of loss. Complex structural losses often take longer while repair scopes are finalized. Most residential water damage claims with straightforward covered causes (burst pipe, appliance failure) and complete documentation resolve faster: 2–4 weeks from filing to settlement offer is typical for Category 1 losses with a cooperating contractor and complete moisture log.

Structural drying for a Category 1 loss in an average residential space typically requires 3–5 days using industrial dehumidifiers and air movers monitored with calibrated moisture meters, per IICRC S500 drying protocols. Colorado Springs's low relative humidity (typically 20–40%) accelerates drying compared to coastal markets, which can shorten equipment rental periods and total restoration cost.

Reconstruction — replacing drywall, flooring, and finishes after drying is complete — requires additional weeks and separate contractor scheduling. The restoration contractor handles the drying phase; general contractors or full-service firms handle rebuild. Ensure both phases are addressed in the adjuster's estimate.

Bottom Line

Filing a water damage insurance claim in Colorado Springs requires three priorities: document everything before remediation begins, start extraction immediately to limit mold risk and meet your policy's mitigation requirements, and work with a contractor who follows IICRC S500 drying protocols and produces daily moisture logs for your adjuster. Burst pipe claims from Colorado's freeze-thaw cycle are common in El Paso County — they're well-understood by local adjusters when properly documented. If your claim is disputed, the Colorado Division of Insurance is a legitimate consumer resource at doi.colorado.gov. Frequently Asked Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover burst pipes in Colorado Springs?

Yes — burst pipes from sudden freezing are generally covered under a standard homeowners policy as sudden and accidental water damage. However, if the home was left unoccupied and the heat was turned off or set below the minimum temperature specified in your policy (some Colorado policies require at least 55°F), the insurer may apply an exclusion. Document the freeze conditions using local NOAA weather data for El Paso County to corroborate your claim date.

Does homeowners insurance cover basement flooding from snowmelt?

No — exterior floodwater, including spring snowmelt that enters a basement from outside, is excluded from standard homeowners policies and requires a separate flood insurance policy through the NFIP or a private flood insurer. This distinction matters in neighborhoods like Fountain and Security-Widefield, which see seasonal snowmelt flooding. If the water came from a burst interior pipe that then flooded the basement, that's a different analysis — the source of the water determines coverage.

Do I need to wait for the adjuster before starting water damage restoration?

No — and waiting can actually violate your policy. Most homeowners insurance policies include a "duty to mitigate" clause requiring you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Mold can begin growing in 24–48 hours on wet organic materials per the IICRC S520 standard. Start extraction immediately, document thoroughly before work begins, and notify your insurer right away. The adjuster will review the contractor's moisture log and scope, not an untouched damage scene.

What is an IICRC S500 moisture log and why does my adjuster need it?

The IICRC S500 Standard and Reference Guide for Professional Water Damage Restoration requires restoration contractors to document daily psychrometric readings — temperature, relative humidity, and moisture content of affected materials — throughout the structural drying process. This log demonstrates that drying was performed to measurable standards, not guessed at. Colorado Springs's low ambient humidity (typically 20–40% RH) means the log may show faster drying than a coastal market — which can reduce equipment days billed and is verifiable evidence of a legitimate scope.

Is sewer backup covered under standard homeowners insurance in Colorado?

Not on a standard policy. Sewer backup — sewage backing up through floor drains, toilets, or basement drains — requires a separate sewer backup endorsement added to your homeowners policy. This is a separate premium line item. Old Colorado City neighborhoods with older plumbing and no backflow prevention have elevated sewer backup risk; checking whether you have this endorsement before a loss is far easier than disputing it after. The Colorado Division of Insurance at doi.colorado.gov can explain endorsement requirements.

What's the difference between a restoration contractor and a public adjuster?

A restoration contractor performs the physical work — water extraction, structural drying, mold prevention — and documents the scope and moisture data for your adjuster. A public adjuster is a licensed claims professional who works for the policyholder (not the insurer) to negotiate the settlement amount. You may need both on a large or disputed loss. Public adjusters are licensed and regulated by the Colorado Division of Insurance and typically charge a percentage of the claim settlement, usually 10–15%.

How does Colorado's low humidity affect water damage restoration costs?

Colorado Springs averages relative humidity of 20–40%, significantly lower than coastal or Midwest markets. This lower ambient humidity accelerates structural drying — industrial dehumidifiers achieve target dryness levels in fewer days than the same equipment would take in a more humid environment. The IICRC S500 drying goals (measured in moisture content of materials, not time) are reached faster, which can reduce equipment rental days billed to your insurer. If a contractor bills for an unusually long drying period without psychrometric data to support it, that's worth questioning.

What happens if my insurance claim is denied or underpaid in Colorado?

You have three main options. First, request a re-inspection with the restoration contractor present to walk through disputed line items directly with the adjuster. Second, invoke the appraisal clause in your policy — most Colorado homeowners policies include this binding dispute mechanism, which is faster and cheaper than litigation. Third, file a complaint with the Colorado Division of Insurance at doi.colorado.gov; the agency reviews insurer conduct under Colorado regulations and is a legitimate regulatory resource for consumers facing improper denials or delays.
