How does the restoration process work for Fountain properties?

The contractors we connect you with follow the IICRC S500 Standard and Reference Guide for Professional Water Damage Restoration. The process moves through four required phases: assessment → extraction → structural drying → documentation. Each phase must be completed before the next begins. Skipping steps is how a water damage claim becomes a mold remediation claim.

  1. Moisture assessment: Technicians use thermal imaging cameras and penetrating moisture meters to map where water has migrated — inside wall cavities, under flooring, and into subfloor assemblies. Water travels farther than visible staining suggests, particularly in the slab-on-grade construction common in Fountain's newer developments near Venetian at Fountain Crossing.
  2. Water extraction: Commercial truck-mounted extraction units remove significantly more water per hour than consumer equipment, pulling water from carpet, pad, and the top layer of subfloor material in a single pass. Speed at this stage directly limits the scope of subsequent drying and demolition.
  3. Structural drying: Industrial dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are positioned according to a drying chamber plan. For Category 1 (clean water) losses in a standard residential footprint, structural drying typically runs 3–5 days per IICRC S500 guidelines. Category 2 losses (gray water) or larger affected areas typically require additional time. Note: Fountain's elevation of approximately 5,500 feet produces lower ambient humidity than Front Range communities at lower elevation, which in most cases supports faster evaporation rates — though this advantage is offset in winter months when structures are sealed and heating systems recirculate dry air that can mask moisture meter readings. Technicians return daily to log moisture readings and adjust equipment placement.
  4. Documentation package: Every moisture reading, equipment placement log, and daily technician note is compiled into a report for your insurance adjuster. This documentation is required for most residential claims under standard carrier protocols and typically accelerates the claims process.
💡 Tip: If you're a property manager handling a Fort Carson-area rental in Fountain, ask the restoration contractor to provide a separate documentation package for the property owner and the tenant file. Insurance adjusters handling military housing-adjacent properties are familiar with the Fort Carson rental market and typically expect detailed prior-damage disclosure — a full moisture log from the current event protects you if undiscovered prior damage surfaces during tearout.

Will insurance cover water damage restoration in Fountain, CO?

Most standard homeowners insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage — a burst pipe, an appliance supply line failure, or a water heater rupture. What they typically exclude: gradual damage from a slow leak the homeowner "knew or should have known" about, exterior floodwater (which requires a separate NFIP or private flood insurance policy), and sewer backup (which requires a specific endorsement that many Fountain policyholders do not carry).

The Colorado Division of Insurance (doi.colorado.gov) handles policy disputes for Colorado policyholders and is the appropriate contact if you believe a claim is being improperly denied. The contractors we connect you with provide complete IICRC-compliant moisture documentation and work directly with your adjuster — coverage determinations rest with the carrier, not the restoration contractor.
