What Makes Security-Widefield's Water Damage Risk Profile Different?

Security-Widefield's housing stock was built primarily between 1960 and 1990, during rapid expansion tied to Fort Carson's growth. This creates three specific water damage vulnerabilities.

Galvanized steel supply lines. Homes built before the mid-1980s commonly feature galvanized steel that corrodes from the inside out. Internal rust buildup restricts flow, increases pressure stress at joints, and eventually causes pinhole leaks or full pipe failures. Because corrosion is invisible, these failures often produce slow leaks inside walls for weeks before becoming visible. By the time a homeowner notices a soft spot in drywall or ceiling stain, IICRC S500 Class 3 moisture conditions—where the entire wall assembly is wet—are frequently already present.

Freeze risk during cold months. Security-Widefield sits at approximately 5,700 feet elevation, slightly lower than central Colorado Springs's 6,000-foot average. Older construction in the area frequently lacks adequate insulation on exterior walls and crawl spaces. During El Paso County's October-through-April freeze season, pipes freeze in garage conversions and utility rooms that were not built to modern insulation standards. Colorado Springs's elevation and freeze cycles significantly extend the risk window compared to lower-elevation communities.

External drainage and flooding. The Security-Widefield Drainage District manages channels that run through the community, including the Security-Widefield Drain along Security Boulevard. Heavy spring snowmelt and intense afternoon thunderstorms (May–August) can cause these channels to back up or overflow into adjacent properties. Floodwater from external drainage channels is Category 3 water under IICRC S500—the most contaminated classification, requiring full removal of affected porous materials rather than in-place drying.

⚠️ Watch out: Floodwater from external sources—whether from the Security-Widefield drainage system, street runoff, or rising water table—is typically not covered by standard homeowners insurance. You need a separate flood insurance policy through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) or a private flood insurer. Many Security-Widefield homeowners discover this gap only after filing a claim.

What Structural Drying Looks Like in a Security-Widefield Home

IICRC S500 governs water damage restoration protocol regardless of geography. The contractor we connect you with applies the same procedures in Security-Widefield as anywhere else in El Paso County:

  1. Water extraction: Commercial truck-mounted or portable extraction units remove standing water. Consumer shop vacuums cannot reach water absorbed into subfloor assemblies or wall cavities.
  2. Category and class assessment: The technician identifies water category (1, 2, or 3 under IICRC S500) and loss class based on volume and affected materials. A burst supply line in a finished basement is typically Category 1, Class 3 or 4. Sewage backup is Category 3 regardless of class.
  3. Material decisions: Category 3 porous materials—drywall, carpet, pad, insulation—must be removed and cannot be dried in place. Category 1 or 2 materials may be candidates for drying depending on loss class and moisture readings.
  4. Structural drying setup: Industrial dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned according to a drying plan. Security-Widefield's 1960s–1980s homes often have original blown-in insulation in walls and lack vapor barriers under slab or crawl space floors—both factors that slow drying.
  5. Daily moisture monitoring: Technicians return daily to log moisture readings across all affected assemblies. Drying is complete when all readings reach IICRC S500 standards—typically 3 to 5 days for Category 1 losses, longer for Category 2 or 3.
  6. Insurance documentation: All moisture readings, equipment placement, and daily logs are compiled for the insurance adjuster. El Paso County claims follow the same adjuster network as Colorado Springs proper—documentation in standard format speeds processing.
