How quickly do I need to call a water damage company?

Quick Answer

Call a water damage company within the first hour — mold can begin growing in 24–48 hours and structural damage compounds every hour water sits.

Key Takeaways

  • The IICRC S500 standard classifies water damage urgency by category and class — Category 3 (sewage, floodwater) requires professional response immediately, with no safe DIY window.
  • Mold can begin growing on wet organic materials in as little as 24–48 hours at room temperature, per the IICRC S520 standard — making same-day response the threshold that separates a drying job from a mold remediation job.
  • Colorado Springs's elevation (~6,000 ft) accelerates surface evaporation but does NOT speed structural drying — water absorbs into drywall and subfloor at the same rate regardless of altitude, while high-altitude drying conditions can mask moisture still trapped in building materials.
  • Most homeowners insurance policies require prompt reporting of water damage — delayed calls can give insurers grounds to argue the damage worsened due to negligence, reducing or denying the claim.

Why does the first hour matter so much after water damage?

Water does not sit still. The moment a pipe bursts, an appliance fails, or a basement floods, water begins moving — laterally through flooring, vertically up drywall through capillary action, and into insulation and framing where no consumer tool can reach it. Every hour without professional extraction and structural drying expands the affected area and the repair bill that follows.

The IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration organizes water losses by category (contamination level) and class (volume and material absorption). Category 1 losses from a clean supply line are the most forgiving, but even clean water can escalate to Category 2 within 24 hours as it absorbs bacteria from building materials. Category 3 losses — sewage backup, toilet overflow, or exterior floodwater — have no safe waiting window. Contamination is present from the first moment of contact.

⚠️ Watch out: If your water damage involves sewage, a backed-up floor drain, or floodwater that entered from outside the structure, this is IICRC Category 3 (black water). Do not attempt extraction with consumer equipment or walk through the affected area without protective gear. Category 3 contaminated materials must be removed — they cannot be dried in place.

When does water damage become a mold problem?

The IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation identifies 24–48 hours as the window within which mold can begin growing on wet organic materials — drywall paper, wood framing, OSB subfloor, carpet backing — at typical indoor temperatures. That threshold determines whether a job stays a water damage job or becomes a combined water damage and mold remediation job, which typically increases total restoration costs significantly.

EPA mold guidance (epa.gov/mold) reinforces this timeline, recommending that wet materials be dried within 24–48 hours to prevent mold growth. For Colorado Springs homeowners, that window closes faster than it appears when you account for the time to discover the damage, assess the source, reach a contractor, and have equipment on site.

💡 Tip: If you discovered water damage this morning and are reading this tonight, you have used most of your mold-prevention window. Call a Colorado Springs restoration contractor now — a same-evening response is better than waiting until morning to "see how it looks."

What does Colorado Springs's climate do to the response timeline?

Colorado Springs sits at approximately 6,035 feet of elevation, creating a drying condition that routinely misleads homeowners. Low relative humidity at altitude means surface materials dry faster than they would in a coastal or humid climate — a wet towel dries in an hour, making it tempting to assume floors and walls are doing the same. They are not.

Structural materials — drywall, OSB, engineered hardwood, concrete block — absorb and release moisture slowly regardless of surface conditions. High-altitude air dries the surface while moisture remains trapped inside wall cavities and subfloor assemblies. Without calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging, a homeowner can believe a wall is dry when it still holds enough moisture for mold growth inside the cavity. This is why IICRC-certified contractors use daily moisture logs rather than visual inspection — surface appearance is not a reliable indicator at any elevation.

Colorado Springs also presents specific seasonal risk patterns that affect how quickly damage escalates:

  • October–April (freeze season): Burst pipes from freeze-thaw cycling are typically the most common winter water loss in El Paso County. Pipes in exterior walls, attached garages, and crawl spaces in older Fountain Valley and Old Colorado City homes are especially vulnerable. Frozen pipe failures often go undetected until the pipe thaws and flow resumes — sometimes hours after the initial freeze event — meaning water has already been running unobserved.
  • March–June (snowmelt season): Rapid snowmelt from the Front Range foothills causes basement flooding in lower-elevation neighborhoods. Briargate and newer north-side developments with finished basements see sump pump failures and foundation seepage during high-melt events. Saturated ground can push water through foundation walls even without a direct plumbing failure.
  • May–August (hail season): Colorado Springs experiences multiple significant hail events per year. Hail punctures or displaces roofing material, allowing water intrusion into attic spaces that then migrates down through ceiling assemblies. This type of loss often goes undetected for days because ceiling damage appears slowly as water wicks downward.

What happens to your insurance claim if you wait too long?

Most standard homeowners insurance policies in Colorado cover sudden and accidental water damage — a burst pipe or appliance failure — but include language requiring the policyholder to take reasonable steps to mitigate further damage after a loss event. Waiting 48 to 72 hours before calling a restoration contractor, particularly when documentation shows you were aware of the damage, can give an adjuster grounds to argue that a portion of the damage resulted from delayed mitigation rather than the original loss.

In practice, insurers typically cover damage caused by the incident — not additional damage caused by inaction afterward. A burst pipe that caused $5,000 in damage may see a portion of the claim disputed if waiting extended the loss to $9,000. Prompt documentation — photos, video, and professional moisture readings on day one — protects the claim. Contractors who work regularly with El Paso County adjusters will begin moisture documentation on arrival, creating the paper trail that supports your adjuster's report.

⚠️ Watch out: Sewer backup and surface water flooding from storms are NOT covered under a standard Colorado homeowners policy. Sewer backup requires a separate endorsement; exterior floodwater requires a separate flood insurance policy (NFIP or private). If your water damage involves either source, call your insurance agent before assuming coverage exists.

Response Timeline: What Happens When You Wait

  • 0–1 hour: Water spreads laterally. Shut off the water source and call a restoration contractor. The faster extraction begins, the smaller the affected area.
  • 1–4 hours: Drywall continues absorbing moisture. Flooring begins to buckle or swell at seams. Insulation in wall cavities begins to saturate. Professional extraction at this stage can still limit structural damage significantly.
  • 4–24 hours: Structural materials are heavily saturated. Drywall may begin to separate. Wood framing absorbs moisture and swelling begins. Drying time and cost both increase.
  • 24–48 hours: Per IICRC S520, mold can begin growing on wet organic materials at room temperature. A water damage job may now require mold remediation as well.
  • 48–72 hours and beyond: Structural compromise in subfloor and framing becomes increasingly likely. Per IICRC S500 guidelines, Category 1 water that has remained in place this long is typically reclassified as Category 2 or Category 3 due to bacterial growth. Remediation scope and cost increase substantially.

What should you do in the minutes before the restoration crew arrives?

Calling a contractor immediately is the most important action. While you wait, some steps are safe to take — and a few are not.

Safe actions before the crew arrives:

  1. Shut off the water source if you can do so safely and the valve is accessible. For a burst pipe, this means the main shutoff. For an appliance failure, use the supply line valve behind the appliance.
  2. Document everything with video and photos before moving anything. Walk the perimeter. Photos taken before cleanup began carry more weight with insurance adjusters than photos taken after.
  3. Move electronics, furniture, and irreplaceable items out of standing water — provided you can do so without stepping into Category 3 water (sewage or outdoor floodwater).
  4. If the loss involves your electrical panel or outlets near the water, do not enter the affected area. Contact your utility provider (Black Hills Energy for most Colorado Springs service areas) to request a safety shutoff before the restoration crew enters.

What not to do:

  • Do not run a consumer shop vac or wet/dry vacuum and assume the area is dry. Consumer equipment removes standing water only — it cannot extract water from inside drywall, subfloor, or insulation. Using a shop vac and delaying professional extraction because "we got most of the water up" is one of the most common causes of mold callbacks in water damage jobs.
  • Do not turn on ceiling fans or HVAC if the loss involves Category 2 or Category 3 water. Airflow can spread contaminants through the structure.
  • Do not enter any area with sewage backup or standing exterior floodwater without protective equipment — gloves, boots, and eye protection at minimum.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it okay to wait until morning to call a water damage company if the flooding happened at night?

No — the IICRC S520 standard cites 24–48 hours as the window in which mold can begin growing on wet organic materials at room temperature, and that clock starts when the water does. A Colorado Springs contractor responding at 2 a.m. will have extraction equipment running by 3 a.m., which is hours earlier than a morning call permits. Most local restoration contractors — including those we connect you with — operate 24/7 for exactly this reason.

What is the difference between Category 1, 2, and 3 water damage, and does it change how fast I need to call?

Per the IICRC S500 standard: Category 1 is clean water from a supply line or drinking water source; Category 2 (gray water) comes from dishwashers, washing machines, or toilet tank overflow; Category 3 (black water) involves sewage, toilet bowl overflow, or exterior floodwater. Category 1 gives you the narrowest window to act before it degrades to Category 2 — and Category 3 requires immediate professional response with no safe DIY window at any point.

Will my homeowners insurance cover water damage if I call right away?

Most standard Colorado homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage — burst pipes and appliance failures are typically covered. However, policies also require you to mitigate further damage promptly. Delaying your call can give the insurer grounds to dispute the portion of damage that occurred after you were aware of the loss. Note that sewer backup and exterior floodwater require separate policy endorsements — standard policies do not cover those.

Does Colorado Springs's high altitude affect how fast water damage spreads?

Altitude affects surface evaporation but not structural absorption. At 6,000 feet, the low relative humidity dries surface materials quickly, which can make a water loss appear less serious than it is. Water absorbed into drywall, OSB subfloor, and wall cavity insulation releases at the same rate regardless of altitude — and moisture meters, not visual inspection, are the only reliable way to confirm structural drying is complete.

How do I know if my basement flooding in Colorado Springs is covered by insurance?

If your basement flooded because a pipe burst or your sump pump failed and your policy has a sump pump endorsement, it is likely covered. If the water entered from outside — through a window well, foundation wall, or surface drainage — that is typically classified as a flood event requiring separate flood insurance (NFIP or private). El Paso County properties in low-lying areas near Fountain Creek or Monument Creek may be in a designated flood zone — check FEMA's flood map service (msc.fema.gov) for your property's flood zone status.

Can I use a shop vac to clean up the water while I wait for the contractor?

You can use a shop vac to remove standing surface water from hard floors, but understand that it removes only what is visible. Water absorbed into drywall, subfloor, carpet padding, and insulation cannot be extracted with consumer equipment. Industrial truck-mounted extraction units generate far greater suction and can draw moisture from within building materials. Running a shop vac and delaying the professional call because the floor looks clear is a common mistake that leads to mold growing inside wall cavities.

How long does structural drying take after a water loss in Colorado Springs?

Per IICRC S500 guidelines, a Category 1 water loss in a well-contained area typically dries in 3–5 days with industrial dehumidifiers and air movers monitored by daily moisture readings. Category 2 or 3 losses, larger affected areas, or losses involving wall cavity saturation take longer. Restoration contractors will establish a drying goal — the target moisture content for each material type — and monitor progress daily until readings confirm the structure has reached that goal.

What are the signs of water damage I might be missing between freeze events in Colorado Springs?

The most common missed signs after Colorado's freeze-thaw cycles include water stains on ceilings directly below exterior rooflines or near exterior wall corners, bubbling or peeling paint on walls adjacent to exterior plumbing chases, a musty smell in closets that share a wall with an exterior pipe run, and unexplained increases in water bills. In Old Colorado City and older Fountain Valley properties with galvanized plumbing, slow pinhole leaks from freeze stress are common and often go undetected for weeks before causing visible damage.
