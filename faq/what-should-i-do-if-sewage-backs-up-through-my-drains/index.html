What should I do if sewage backs up through my drains?

Quick Answer

A sewage backup is Category 3 black water — evacuate affected areas immediately, avoid contact, and call a certified restoration contractor; do not attempt cleanup yourself.

Key Takeaways

  • Sewage backup is classified as Category 3 (black water) under the IICRC S500 standard, meaning it contains pathogens and cannot be dried in place — all contaminated porous materials must be removed.
  • Mold can begin growing on wet organic materials within 24–48 hours per the IICRC S520 standard, making same-day professional response critical after a sewage event.
  • Standard homeowners insurance policies in Colorado Springs typically exclude sewer backup — a separate sewer backup endorsement is required, and most El Paso County homeowners don't realize this gap exists until they file a claim.
  • Colorado Springs's aging sewer infrastructure in older neighborhoods like Old Colorado City, combined with spring snowmelt surging through municipal lines, makes sewage backups more common March through May than any other period.

Why Is Sewage Backup Classified Differently From Other Water Damage?

Not all water damage is equal, and sewage backup sits at the most serious end of the scale. Under the IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration, water is classified into three categories based on contamination level. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line. Category 2 is gray water — contaminated but not sewage. Category 3, which includes sewage backup, is called black water.

Category 3 water contains bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites at concentrations that pose a direct health risk to anyone who contacts it. This includes sewage backing up through floor drains, toilets, or tub drains, as well as floodwater entering from outside through storm drains or municipal overflow. The IICRC S500 standard is explicit: porous materials — drywall, insulation, carpet, wood flooring, subflooring, and framing — contaminated by Category 3 water cannot be dried and saved. They must be removed, bagged, and disposed of according to local regulations.

This is not a conservative interpretation. Pathogens embed themselves in porous materials at a cellular level, and no drying process eliminates microbial contamination from organic building materials.

⚠️ Watch out: Consumer shop vacs, mops, and household disinfectants are not adequate for sewage cleanup. Using them without protective equipment risks direct pathogen exposure, and incomplete removal of contaminated material will result in mold growth and continued health hazards inside your home.

What Should I Do Immediately After a Sewage Backup?

The first minutes after discovering sewage in your drains matter, but the priority is safety — not cleanup.

Immediate Steps After a Sewage Backup

  • Stop using all plumbing in the home. Running water anywhere — toilets, sinks, showers — adds to the backup volume and spreads contamination further.
  • Evacuate the affected area. Do not walk through standing sewage. If it has reached electrical outlets, panels, or appliances, turn off power to affected circuits at the breaker — but only if you can do so without entering contaminated water.
  • Ventilate if possible. Open windows in unaffected areas to reduce airborne pathogen concentration. Do not run HVAC systems — ductwork can distribute contaminated air throughout the home.
  • Do not attempt to clean it yourself. Category 3 remediation requires full personal protective equipment (PPE), commercial extraction equipment, EPA-registered antimicrobial agents, and proper waste disposal per Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE) guidelines.
  • Document everything before anything is touched. Photograph and video the full extent of contamination — every affected room, material, and drain. This documentation is the foundation of your insurance claim.
  • Call a certified restoration contractor immediately. Contact us at (719) 555-0100 — we connect you with IICRC-certified Colorado Springs contractors available 24/7.
  • Notify your insurance company. Report the loss the same day. Ask specifically about your sewer backup endorsement (see insurance section below).

What Does Professional Sewage Cleanup Actually Involve?

The IICRC S500 standard defines a specific protocol for Category 3 water remediation that goes well beyond extraction and drying. Here is what certified contractors will actually do on a sewage job in Colorado Springs:

Containment and Protection

Technicians set up containment barriers before beginning work to prevent cross-contamination to unaffected areas of the home. This is particularly important in Colorado Springs homes with open-plan basements, where a floor drain backup can be adjacent to finished living space. Full PPE — respirators, chemical-resistant suits, gloves, and eye protection — is required by IICRC S500 protocol for all personnel in the work area.

Extraction of Standing Contaminated Water

Commercial truck-mounted extraction units remove standing sewage. These operate at a different scale than consumer wet-vacs — the vacuum pressure and tank capacity allow for rapid, thorough extraction that leaves behind far less residual liquid than portable units. All extracted material is treated as biohazardous waste.

Removal of All Contaminated Porous Materials

Per the IICRC S500 standard, any porous material that has been in contact with Category 3 water must be removed. In a typical Colorado Springs basement sewage backup, this commonly includes carpet and carpet pad, drywall to at least 12 inches above the water line, insulation, and in some cases oriented strand board (OSB) subflooring. Hardwood floors are typically unsalvageable after a sewage event. The goal is to leave only non-porous, cleanable surfaces — concrete slab, metal framing, PVC pipe.

Antimicrobial Treatment

All remaining structural surfaces are treated with EPA-registered antimicrobial agents. This is not a precaution — it is a required protocol step under the IICRC S500 standard. The EPA maintains guidance on sewage contamination and health risks that aligns with this requirement. Surfaces are treated, allowed to dwell for the required contact time, and then moisture-tested before any rebuild begins.

Structural Drying and Moisture Verification

After material removal and antimicrobial treatment, industrial dehumidifiers and air movers establish a controlled drying environment. Technicians take daily moisture readings with calibrated meters and log them. At Colorado Springs's elevation of approximately 6,035 feet, lower ambient humidity can assist evaporative drying — but equipment calibrated for lower-elevation jobs may perform differently, and dehumidifiers require closer monitoring as a result. Drying for a Category 3 loss typically runs longer than a clean-water event — in most cases 5–7 days for structural components, depending on scope and ambient conditions.

💡 Tip: Ask the contractor to provide daily moisture logs in writing. This documentation is required by most insurance adjusters to verify that drying was completed to IICRC standards before rebuild — without it, your claim may face delays or disputes.

Mold Risk After Sewage Backup

The IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation notes that mold can begin growing on wet organic materials within 24–48 hours at typical indoor temperatures. Sewage events carry elevated mold risk because the organic material in sewage accelerates colonization. Even after proper Category 3 remediation, moisture left in wall cavities, under flooring, or in framing creates conditions where mold growth is likely. Moisture verification — not visual inspection alone — is a mandatory final step under IICRC protocol before any reconstruction begins. The IICRC provides public resources on S500 and S520 standards for homeowners who want to review the full protocol.

Why Are Sewage Backups More Common in Colorado Springs Than Homeowners Expect?

Colorado Springs has a specific combination of infrastructure, geography, and seasonal patterns that make sewage backups more frequent here than in many comparable-sized cities.

Spring Snowmelt Surging Municipal Lines

El Paso County accumulates significant snowpack through winter at elevation. When temperatures rise rapidly in March and April, large volumes of meltwater enter the ground and storm sewer system simultaneously. In older parts of the city — particularly Old Colorado City and neighborhoods near Monument Creek — combined sewer lines can become overwhelmed during peak snowmelt events. When municipal lines reach or exceed capacity, sewage backs up through the path of least resistance: floor drains and low-lying fixtures in connected homes. This is a municipal surcharge event, and it matters for your insurance claim because coverage rules differ depending on whether the backup originated from your private lateral line or a municipal surcharge.

Aging Infrastructure and Older Plumbing

Homes in Old Colorado City and portions of the Near Northside were built with galvanized or cast-iron drain lines that are now 50–80 years old. Root intrusion into clay sewer laterals is common in these neighborhoods, particularly after freeze-thaw cycles crack pipe joints. Colorado Springs sits in a freeze season running October through April at 6,035 feet, and each freeze-thaw cycle applies mechanical stress to buried pipe joints. A root-intruded lateral that drains adequately in summer can back up completely when spring snowmelt pushes flow to capacity.

Military Housing and Property Management

The Fort Carson and Peterson Space Force Base rental market drives a high volume of property-manager-operated units in Fountain, Security-Widefield, and portions of southeast Colorado Springs. Slow drain issues in these units often go unreported — partly due to transient living arrangements and partly because tenants assume the landlord is aware. By the time a sewage backup is reported, the lateral blockage has typically been developing for weeks. Property managers overseeing these portfolios should establish a protocol for annual sewer camera inspection, particularly on homes built before 1990.

Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Sewage Backup in Colorado Springs?

This is where many Colorado Springs homeowners face an unexpected financial hit. Standard homeowners insurance policies — whether issued by State Farm, USAA, Farmers, or local Colorado carriers — do not include sewer backup coverage by default. It is a separate endorsement, typically called a "water backup and sump overflow" rider, that must be added to the policy.

Without that endorsement, your standard policy will likely not cover a sewer backup event. This applies even when the backup was caused by a municipal line surcharge outside your control. Coverage depends on your specific policy language, not the cause of the backup.

There are exceptions and nuances:

  • If the backup resulted from a sudden, accidental failure of a private drain pipe — rather than root intrusion or gradual blockage — some policies may provide limited coverage under the sudden-and-accidental provision. This requires adjuster review of the specific facts.
  • If the municipal sewer system is responsible for the surcharge, you may have a subrogation claim against the city — but these claims require documentation and legal support, not just a contractor's report.
  • USAA policyholders in the Fort Carson area: USAA's standard policies have specific water backup provisions that differ from civilian carriers — confirm your coverage details with your USAA agent before assuming you are covered or excluded.
⚠️ Watch out: Do not delay notifying your insurance company while you sort out coverage questions. Most policies require prompt notification of a loss — delays can be used to limit or deny claims. Report the loss the same day, even if you are unsure of your coverage.

The contractors we connect you with provide detailed written documentation — photographs, moisture logs, scope of work, and material removal inventories — formatted for insurance adjuster review. Complete documentation is necessary for any claim to be evaluated fairly; whether your claim is approved depends on your policy terms.

How Do I Choose a Sewage Cleanup Contractor in Colorado Springs?

After a sewage backup, you will likely receive calls from multiple contractors — some local, some out-of-area operators who follow disaster events. Verify the following before allowing anyone to begin work:

What to Verify Before Hiring a Sewage Cleanup Contractor

  • IICRC certification: Ask specifically for the technician's IICRC Water Damage Restoration Technician (WRT) certification. For sewage jobs, Applied Microbial Remediation Technician (AMRT) certification is the relevant additional credential. Ask to see the certificate — not just a verbal claim.
  • Colorado contractor license: Restoration contractors performing structural work in El Paso County must hold a valid Colorado contractor license. Verify at the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA) online license lookup before work begins.
  • El Paso County building permits: Structural repairs following sewage remediation — including drywall replacement, subfloor replacement, and framing repair — require permits in El Paso County. A contractor who proposes to skip permits is either unaware of local requirements or cutting corners. Unpermitted structural work can complicate both a future home sale and your insurance claim.
  • Written scope of work before authorization: Obtain a written scope of work that specifies what materials will be removed, what equipment will be used, how long drying is expected to take, and how moisture verification will be documented. Do not authorize work on a verbal estimate.
  • Local presence: After major snowmelt or storm events, out-of-area contractors arrive in Colorado Springs and leave once the surge passes. Verify that the contractor has a local address and local phone number — not a call center that routes to national dispatch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is sewage backup covered by my Colorado Springs homeowners insurance?

Standard homeowners insurance policies typically do not cover sewage backup — it requires a separate "water backup and sump overflow" endorsement added to your policy. If you don't have this endorsement, you are likely responsible for out-of-pocket costs, which commonly range from $7,000–$15,000 for a typical Colorado Springs basement sewage event. Verify your specific policy language with your insurance agent today.

Can I clean up a sewage backup myself?

No — sewage is classified as Category 3 black water under the IICRC S500 standard, containing bacteria, viruses, and parasites at concentrations that pose direct health risks. All contaminated porous materials, including drywall, carpet, insulation, and wood subflooring, must be professionally removed and disposed of per Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE) guidelines. Consumer cleaning products and shop vacs are not adequate for this contamination level.

How long does sewage cleanup take in Colorado Springs?

A typical residential sewage backup remediation — extraction, material removal, antimicrobial treatment, and structural drying — takes 5–10 days depending on the scope of contamination and materials affected. Structural drying alone for a Category 3 loss typically runs 5–7 days, verified by daily moisture meter readings per the IICRC S500 protocol. Reconstruction (drywall, flooring, paint) begins only after moisture readings confirm drying is complete.

Why does my basement floor drain back up every spring in Colorado Springs?

Spring snowmelt surging through El Paso County's municipal sewer system is the most common cause of seasonal floor drain backups in Colorado Springs. Rapid snowmelt in March and April overwhelms older combined sewer lines — particularly in neighborhoods near Monument Creek and Old Colorado City — causing sewage to back up into homes through the lowest fixtures. Annual sewer camera inspection of your lateral line can identify root intrusion or blockages before the spring surge season.

Do I need a permit for sewage cleanup repairs in El Paso County?

Yes — structural repairs following sewage remediation, including drywall replacement, subfloor replacement, and any framing repair, require building permits in El Paso County. Many homeowners and even some out-of-area contractors are unaware of this requirement. Unpermitted structural work can complicate your home sale and create issues with your insurance claim if discovered later.

What is Category 3 water, and why does it matter?

Category 3, or black water, is the IICRC S500 classification for the most severely contaminated water — including sewage, toilet bowl overflow, and exterior floodwater. It matters because Category 3 material cannot be dried in place: all porous building materials that have contacted it must be removed and disposed of, not dried and saved as you might do with a Category 1 clean-water pipe burst. Attempting to dry Category 3 material leaves behind embedded pathogens and creates conditions for mold growth.

Can mold grow after a sewage backup even if I clean it up quickly?

Yes — the IICRC S520 standard notes that mold can begin growing on wet organic materials within 24–48 hours at room temperature. Sewage events accelerate mold colonization because organic material in sewage acts as a nutrient source. Even after professional remediation, any residual moisture left in wall cavities, under flooring, or in framing can lead to mold growth, which is why moisture verification with calibrated meters — not visual inspection alone — is a required final step before reconstruction.

What is the difference between a sewer backup from my lateral line versus a municipal surcharge?

A lateral line backup originates from a blockage or failure in the private sewer pipe connecting your home to the city main — typically caused by root intrusion, grease buildup, or pipe collapse. A municipal surcharge occurs when the city's main sewer line becomes overwhelmed and sewage backs up into homes through connected laterals. The distinction matters for insurance and liability: a lateral failure may be covered under your sewer backup endorsement, while a municipal surcharge may support a claim against the City of Colorado Springs — but both require immediate professional remediation regardless of the source.
