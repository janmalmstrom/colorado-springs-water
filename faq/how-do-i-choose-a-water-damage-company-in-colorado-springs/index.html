How do I choose a water damage company in Colorado Springs?

Quick Answer

Choose a water damage company in Colorado Springs by verifying three things before you sign anything: IICRC certification (the industry standard set by the Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification), an active Colorado contractor license you can confirm through the Colorado Division of Insurance at doi.colorado.gov, and documented local experience with El Paso County's specific loss patterns — burst pipes from freeze-thaw cycles, spring snowmelt basement flooding, and hail-driven roof intrusion. A company that meets all three criteria and offers free on-site moisture readings is worth the call.

Start With IICRC Certification — And Know What It Actually Means

The IICRC — Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification — publishes the technical standards that define how water damage restoration is supposed to be performed. The two you need to know are S500 (Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration) and S520 (Standard for Professional Mold Remediation). When a company says its technicians are IICRC certified, that means they've been trained and tested against those specific standards — not a general contractor's license, not an online course, not brand affiliation.

You can verify IICRC certification directly at iicrc.org. Search the contractor's company name or individual technician names. If they claim certification but don't appear in the directory, ask for the certificate number. A legitimate company will provide it without hesitation. IICRC certification is voluntary — which means a contractor who pursues it is choosing to be held to a higher standard. That matters when you're deciding who gets inside your home at 2 a.m. after a pipe bursts.

Verify the Colorado Contractor License — Don't Skip This Step

Colorado requires contractors performing restoration work to carry proper licensing. Before you authorize any work, confirm the company's license is active through the Colorado Division of Insurance. An unlicensed contractor performing structural work — removing drywall, replacing subfloor, opening walls — creates liability problems if your insurer requires licensed contractor documentation for the claim, and it leaves you with no recourse if the work is done incorrectly.

Ask for proof of general liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage as well. If a technician is injured on your property and the company lacks workers' comp, you may be exposed. A legitimate restoration company carries both and will provide certificates of insurance before starting work. This is a standard ask — any company that resists it is telling you something important.

Evaluate Their Equipment and Process — Not Just Their Sales Pitch

The difference between a professional water damage restoration company and a company with a wet-vac and a dehumidifier from a hardware store is not cosmetic — it determines whether your structure actually dries. Per the IICRC S500 standard, proper structural drying requires commercial-grade equipment calibrated to the specific conditions of your loss: the water category, the materials affected, and the ambient temperature and humidity. Ask these specific questions before hiring:

  • Do you use truck-mounted extraction units? Commercial truck-mounted extractors remove significantly more water than portable units — they matter most in the first hours.
  • How do you track drying progress? The answer should include daily moisture meter readings logged to a drying report. This documentation goes to your insurance adjuster as proof that drying was completed to standard.
  • What psychrometric data do you collect? Structural drying is a science involving temperature, relative humidity, and dew point — not just running equipment until things feel dry. Technicians should be recording this data.
  • What is the projected drying timeline? Category 1 (clean water) losses typically require 3–5 days of active structural drying. Category 2 (gray water) and Category 3 (black water/sewage) take longer and involve additional decontamination protocols.

If a company can't answer these questions specifically, they are not following IICRC S500 protocol.

Understand Colorado Springs-Specific Risk — Local Experience Matters

Not all water damage companies have equal familiarity with El Paso County's specific loss patterns. Colorado Springs sits at approximately 6,000 feet of elevation, which means freeze risk runs from October through April — longer and more severe than lower-elevation Colorado markets. Pipes in exterior walls, garages, and uninsulated crawl spaces are particularly vulnerable to the rapid freeze-thaw cycles common here. A company that primarily handles humid-climate water losses may not recognize the signs of freeze damage in materials that are less commonly affected at lower elevations.

Spring snowmelt is a second distinct pattern: rapid melt combined with saturated soil drives water into basements, particularly in neighborhoods like Briargate where sump pump capacity can be overwhelmed. Hailstorms — Colorado Springs averages five or more significant hail events per year — create a "hail-to-water path": roof damage allows water intrusion into attic space, which then wicks into ceiling materials before the homeowner notices anything. A company with genuine local history recognizes these patterns and knows how to assess for secondary damage that out-of-area storm chasers often miss.

After a major storm event, out-of-area contractors arrive in force and leave when the work runs out. A local company has established relationships with El Paso County building inspectors, local insurance adjusters, and subcontractors. If a problem surfaces three months after remediation, they are still here.

Know What to Expect From Insurance Documentation

Most homeowners insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage — a burst pipe, an appliance failure, an ice dam. They typically do not cover gradual damage (a slow leak that went unaddressed) or exterior floodwater, which requires separate flood insurance through the NFIP or a private carrier. Sewer backup coverage usually requires a specific endorsement that many homeowners don't realize they need until after the loss.

A professional restoration company should be able to work directly with your adjuster — providing moisture logs, drying reports, and damage documentation in a format the adjuster can use to process the claim. What they cannot do, and should not promise, is guarantee claim approval or specific payout amounts. Coverage decisions are made by your insurer based on your policy. Be cautious of any contractor who promises to "get you a certain dollar amount" from the insurance company — that is not how the claims process works and it is a red flag. What you want is a company that documents the loss thoroughly and accurately, giving your adjuster what they need to evaluate the claim fairly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does IICRC certified mean for a water damage company?
IICRC stands for the Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification. It is the industry body that publishes the S500 standard for water damage restoration and the S520 standard for mold remediation. A company with IICRC-certified technicians has trained and tested personnel against those standards — it is the most widely recognized technical credential in the restoration industry. You can verify certification at iicrc.org.

What is the difference between Category 1, 2, and 3 water damage?
The IICRC S500 standard classifies water losses by contamination level. Category 1 is clean water from supply lines or drinking water sources — lowest risk. Category 2 is gray water from dishwashers, washing machines, or toilet tank overflow — contaminated, requires antimicrobial treatment. Category 3 is black water including sewage, toilet bowl overflow, and exterior floodwater — highest contamination level, requiring removal of affected porous materials rather than drying them in place. The category affects both the remediation protocol and the equipment required.
Why does Colorado Springs have more burst pipe damage than other cities?
Colorado Springs sits at approximately 6,000 feet of elevation, which extends the freeze risk window from October through April. The freeze-thaw cycles at this elevation are more frequent and more severe than in lower-elevation Colorado markets. Pipes in exterior walls, garages, crawl spaces, and older homes without adequate insulation are particularly vulnerable. Spring snowmelt creates a separate but related risk: rapid melt and saturated soil can overwhelm sump pumps and drive water into basements.
Can a water damage company guarantee my insurance claim will be approved?
No legitimate company can guarantee claim approval or a specific payout amount. Coverage decisions are made by your insurer based on your specific policy terms. What a professional restoration company can do is document the loss thoroughly — moisture readings, daily drying logs, damage photographs, and material inventories — in a format your adjuster can use to evaluate the claim. Be cautious of any contractor who promises specific insurance outcomes; that is a significant red flag.
What equipment should a professional water damage company use?
Per the IICRC S500 standard, professional water damage restoration requires commercial-grade extraction equipment — ideally truck-mounted units — followed by industrial dehumidifiers and air movers calibrated to the specific conditions of the loss. Technicians should also use moisture meters and psychrometric instruments to track drying progress and produce a daily drying log. Consumer-grade wet vacuums and hardware-store dehumidifiers cannot achieve the air movement and dehumidification rates required for structural drying to IICRC standard.

A locally operated company has established relationships with El Paso County building inspectors, local insurance adjusters, and regional subcontractors — relationships that matter when permits are required for structural repairs or when follow-up issues arise. Local companies also have direct experience with Colorado Springs-specific loss patterns: freeze-thaw pipe failures, spring snowmelt basement flooding, and hail-driven roof intrusion. After major storms, out-of-area contractors arrive in volume and leave when work runs out; a local company will still be reachable if problems surface months later.
