Should I call my insurance company or a water damage company first?

By Sarah Mitchell
Quick Answer

Call a water damage contractor first — stopping active damage takes priority, and documentation from professionals strengthens your insurance claim.

Key Takeaways

  • Water damage worsens by the hour — mold can begin growing within 24–48 hours on wet organic materials per the IICRC S520 standard, so extraction cannot wait for adjuster scheduling.
  • A contractor's moisture readings and daily drying logs become the core documentation your insurance adjuster uses to assess the claim — calling the contractor first produces better evidence.
  • Most Colorado Springs homeowners insurance covers sudden and accidental water damage (burst pipes, appliance failures) but not gradual leaks or exterior floodwater — knowing the difference before you call affects what you say.
  • Calling your insurer before stopping damage can, in rare cases, be used by adjusters to argue you failed to mitigate — Colorado insurance law and most policies require the policyholder to take reasonable steps to prevent further loss.

Why the sequence matters when water is actively damaging your home

When you walk into standing water in your Colorado Springs home, your instinct may be to call your insurance company immediately. That instinct is understandable — you're worried about coverage and cost. But your policy almost certainly contains a duty to mitigate clause, which requires you to take reasonable steps to prevent the damage from getting worse. Waiting for an adjuster to schedule a visit before extraction begins can complicate your claim, not protect it.

The IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration — the technical standard most insurance carriers recognize — establishes that water damage progresses in predictable, time-sensitive stages. Within the first few hours, drywall, insulation, and subfloor materials are actively absorbing water. By 24–48 hours, per the IICRC S520 standard, mold can begin growing on wet organic materials at typical indoor temperatures. At Colorado Springs' 6,035-foot elevation, forced-air heating systems dry surface air but do not extract moisture from inside wall cavities and subfloor assemblies. The damage is happening where you cannot see it.

The right call sequence is: stop the water source → call a local restoration contractor → then call your insurance company, ideally with preliminary moisture readings in hand.

The correct call sequence for Colorado Springs water damage

  • Step 1: Stop the water source if you can — shut off the main water supply valve or appliance shutoff
  • Step 2: Call a licensed local water damage restoration contractor — same-day emergency response, 24/7
  • Step 3: Let the contractor begin moisture assessment and document readings before extraction
  • Step 4: Call your insurance company to open a claim — use the contractor's initial moisture report as your documentation baseline
  • Step 5: Request your adjuster's contact information and ask whether they want a preferred vendor or will accept your contractor's documentation

What happens when you call insurance first and wait

Insurance adjusters are not restoration technicians. Their job is to assess the value of damage after the fact — not to stop it from progressing. When homeowners call their insurer first and wait for an adjuster before beginning extraction, the damage typically worsens, and the claim becomes harder to document accurately because the initial moisture conditions have changed.

Adjusters in the El Paso County market typically manage high claim volumes after freeze events or spring snowmelt storms. Response times for an in-person adjuster visit typically range from 24 to 72 hours — or longer after a widespread storm. During that window, water in your subfloor wicks into floor joists, water in your wall cavities saturates insulation and reaches electrical boxes, and conditions for mold develop. The IICRC S500 standard categorizes drying as time-critical precisely because these material changes are not reversible once they progress far enough.

A documentation problem also emerges. A contractor who arrives on day one establishes baseline moisture readings with calibrated meters — readings that show exactly how saturated your materials were at the start. If you wait until day two or three, that baseline is lost. Your adjuster works from reconstruction rather than primary evidence.

⚠️ Watch out: Telling your insurance company that you waited before beginning mitigation because you were waiting for their authorization can trigger a coverage dispute. Most standard homeowners policies in Colorado require the insured to take reasonable steps to protect property from further damage. Document every action you take and when you took it.

What to tell your insurance company when you do call

Once a contractor is on-site and has begun preliminary moisture assessment, call your insurer with specific, accurate information. The quality of information you provide in the first call sets the tone for the entire claim.

  • Date and time you discovered the damage
  • Water source — burst pipe, appliance failure, toilet overflow, or storm intrusion (this determines what coverage applies)
  • Approximate affected area — square footage, which rooms, which floor levels
  • Water category if you know it: Category 1 (clean water from a supply line), Category 2 (gray water from an appliance), or Category 3 (sewage or exterior floodwater) — per IICRC S500 classification
  • Steps already taken — when you shut off the water, when the contractor arrived, and that moisture readings have been logged
  • Contractor name and contact — your adjuster will need to coordinate with them

Do not speculate about coverage on the first call. State the facts of what happened. Let your adjuster determine what your policy covers. Describing storm-related basement flooding as a "burst pipe" to sound more covered, for example, can create a material misrepresentation problem later.

💡 Tip: Colorado Springs experiences rapid spring snowmelt events, particularly in March and April, that push water into basements through window wells and foundation cracks. This type of water intrusion is typically classified as flood damage — not covered by standard homeowners insurance. A separate flood insurance policy through the NFIP or a private carrier is required. If your basement flooded during a snowmelt or rainstorm, flag this immediately with your adjuster so coverage questions are addressed upfront rather than after restoration work is underway.

Does calling a contractor first affect your insurance claim?

Some homeowners worry that calling a contractor before their insurance company will somehow damage their claim. In practice, the opposite is typically true. Insurance carriers prefer documented, professionally managed claims. A contractor who follows IICRC S500 protocols produces daily moisture logs, psychrometric readings, equipment placement records, and before-and-after documentation. That paper trail is what adjusters use to validate the scope of damage and approve payment for restoration work.

What occasionally creates friction is hiring a contractor who uses high-pressure tactics, inflates scope, or begins demolition before the adjuster has inspected. Reputable local contractors in the Colorado Springs area know the El Paso County adjuster community and understand that documentation — not aggressive scope inflation — moves claims forward. When you connect with a contractor, ask them directly: do you work directly with insurance adjusters, and do you provide daily moisture logs? Any qualified contractor will answer yes to both.

If your insurer has a preferred vendor list, you are generally not required to use those vendors — Colorado law gives policyholders the right to choose their own licensed contractor. However, using an insurer's preferred vendor may streamline the billing and documentation process in some cases. Ask your adjuster for specifics before making a decision.

Colorado Springs freeze and snowmelt context: why timing is more critical here

Colorado Springs sits at approximately 6,035 feet elevation, with serious freeze risk from October through April. Burst pipe events here have a distinct character compared to lower-elevation markets: pipes in exterior walls, attached garages, and crawl spaces can freeze and fail in a single overnight cold snap, and the volume of water released before discovery can be substantial — often while the homeowner is asleep or away at work.

In the Briargate neighborhood in north Colorado Springs, for example, many 2000s-era homes have finished basements with extensive drywall finishes and engineered hardwood or laminate floors. These materials absorb water rapidly and are significantly more expensive to restore than bare concrete. An overnight pipe failure in Briargate in February can produce 12–18 hours of undetected water flow. By the time the homeowner discovers the loss, the water has already moved through subfloor assemblies and into framing — IICRC S500 Class 3 loss territory, which requires the most aggressive drying protocol.

Every hour of delay in that scenario adds material cost and drying time. Colorado Springs restoration contractors who specialize in freeze-related losses understand the local freeze-thaw cycle, the common pipe failure points in El Paso County home construction, and the drying conditions specific to high-altitude indoor environments. That local knowledge matters both for restoration quality and for accurate claim documentation.

Bottom Line

In a Colorado Springs water damage emergency, call a licensed local restoration contractor first — before calling your insurance company. Stopping active damage and establishing documented moisture baselines protects both your home and your claim. The IICRC S500 standard exists because water damage is time-critical; mold can begin growing in 24–48 hours per the IICRC S520 standard. Your insurer cannot stop that clock — a contractor can. Once extraction is underway, call your insurance company with accurate, specific information about the loss. Frequently Asked Questions

Will my insurance company be upset if I call a water damage contractor before calling them?

No — most homeowners insurance policies in Colorado require you to take reasonable steps to mitigate further damage, which means beginning extraction promptly. Delaying mitigation while waiting for adjuster authorization can actually create coverage complications. Call a contractor to stop the damage, then call your insurer with documented moisture readings in hand.

Does calling a contractor first mean I'm committing to paying out of pocket?

No. Starting mitigation before the adjuster visits does not affect your coverage eligibility. Provide your contractor with your insurance carrier name and claim number once you have it — most restoration contractors in Colorado Springs bill the insurer directly and coordinate documentation with your adjuster as part of the standard process.

What if my insurer says I have to use their preferred vendor?

In Colorado, policyholders have the right to choose their own licensed contractor. Your insurer may recommend a preferred vendor, but they generally cannot require you to use one. If you choose an independent contractor, ensure they follow IICRC S500 standards and produce daily moisture logs — this documentation is what your adjuster needs regardless of who performs the work.

How does a water damage contractor document the damage for my insurance claim?

IICRC S500-compliant contractors use calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging to establish baseline readings, then produce daily psychrometric logs (temperature, humidity, moisture content) throughout the drying period. This documentation shows the adjuster the initial severity of the loss, the equipment deployed, and the progress of drying — it is the primary evidence used to validate the scope and cost of the claim.

What if my basement flooded from a storm or spring snowmelt — is that covered?

Probably not under standard homeowners insurance. Exterior floodwater entering through window wells, foundation cracks, or overwhelmed drainage is typically classified as flood damage, which requires a separate flood insurance policy through the NFIP or a private carrier. Colorado Springs spring snowmelt events are a common cause of this type of loss, and it's important to accurately describe the water source to your adjuster upfront to avoid coverage disputes later.

Does sewer backup require a different type of coverage?

Yes. Sewage backup — classified as Category 3 water under the IICRC S500 standard — is typically excluded from standard homeowners policies and requires a separate sewer backup endorsement. If your toilet overflowed with sewage, a floor drain backed up, or a city sewer line surcharge pushed waste into your home, flag this immediately with your adjuster. Category 3 restoration is also more extensive because contaminated materials must be removed rather than dried.

How long will my insurance company take to send an adjuster after I call?

In normal conditions, El Paso County adjusters typically schedule within 24–72 hours. After a widespread freeze event or spring storm that generates high claim volume across Colorado Springs, that timeline can extend. This is why waiting for adjuster authorization before beginning extraction is not advisable — the drying clock is running regardless of adjuster scheduling.

Can mold really start growing that quickly after water damage?

Yes. The IICRC S520 Standard for Professional Mold Remediation cites 24–48 hours as the window in which mold can begin growing on wet organic materials (drywall, wood framing, insulation) at typical indoor temperatures. This is not a worst-case scenario — it is the standard threshold used by the restoration industry and referenced by the EPA in their mold guidance. Prompt extraction and structural drying are the most effective way to prevent a water damage claim from expanding into a mold remediation claim.
