Should I call my insurance company or a water damage company first?

By Sarah Mitchell
Quick Answer

Call a water damage contractor first — stopping active damage takes priority, and a good contractor documents everything your insurer needs anyway.

Key Takeaways

  • Every hour of delay worsens damage: the IICRC S500 standard recognizes that wet drywall, insulation, and subfloor begin absorbing water within minutes of contact.
  • Mold can begin growing in 24–48 hours on wet organic materials at room temperature, per the IICRC S520 standard — faster mitigation means lower total claim cost.
  • Calling a contractor first does not jeopardize your insurance claim — restoration contractors produce moisture logs and damage documentation that adjusters accept and often prefer.
  • In Colorado Springs, freeze-related pipe failures can continue causing damage if the source isn't located fast — a restoration crew identifies active sources that a claims call cannot.

Why does the order actually matter?

When water actively damages your home, time spent on hold with an insurance company is time water spends soaking into subfloors, wall cavities, and insulation. The IICRC S500 standard — the technical standard for professional water damage restoration — explicitly states that mitigation speed determines the scope of damage. Every hour you delay extraction and drying, the damage category and extent typically grows.

Most homeowners ask: "If I call a contractor before my insurer, will that hurt my claim?" The answer, in the vast majority of cases, is no. Your policy requires you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Calling a restoration contractor fulfills that duty — it does not violate it. Delaying mitigation while waiting for adjuster approval can give an insurer grounds to reduce a claim if damage worsens during that wait.

The Emergency Sequence That Protects Your Property and Your Claim

  • Step 1 — Stop the source: Shut off the main water supply if it's a pipe or appliance failure. If the source is outside floodwater, focus on entry points.
  • Step 2 — Call a water damage contractor: Get extraction and drying underway. The clock on mold growth starts now.
  • Step 3 — Document yourself: Take photos and video of all visible damage before anything is moved or dried. More documentation is always better.
  • Step 4 — Call your insurance company: Report the claim. By this point the contractor will already be generating moisture readings and damage logs — exactly what your adjuster will need.
  • Step 5 — Request a written scope: Ask the restoration contractor for a written damage scope and daily moisture logs. These become the backbone of your claim file.

What does a restoration contractor do that helps your insurance claim?

A qualified restoration contractor produces a paper trail that insurance adjusters are trained to read. Upon arrival, contractors arrive with calibrated moisture meters and thermal imaging equipment. They take baseline moisture readings in affected walls, floors, and ceilings before anything is touched. Those readings are logged daily throughout the drying period and submitted as part of the claim documentation.

Insurance adjusters need to verify two things: the scope of damage at the time of loss, and that mitigation followed industry standards. A contractor following IICRC S500 protocol produces exactly that documentation. An adjuster arriving days later to a dried-out, undocumented room has far less to work with — and undocumented claims are more likely to be disputed or reduced.

💡 Tip: Ask the restoration contractor whether they document moisture readings daily and whether those logs are formatted for insurance claim submission. Contractors familiar with El Paso County adjusters know exactly what documentation format is expected — and that familiarity speeds up your claim cycle.

Does Colorado Springs's climate change the urgency?

Yes. Two specific factors make calling a contractor quickly even more critical here than in many other markets.

Freeze-related pipe failures (October–April)

Colorado Springs sits at 6,035 feet elevation, where the freeze-thaw cycle is aggressive. A pipe that freezes overnight can fail at a point in the wall that isn't immediately visible — meaning water may have been running for hours before you notice pooling. When the source is a frozen pipe, a restoration crew locates where the pipe failed, confirms whether the source is fully shut off, and begins extracting water from wall cavities that homeowners typically cannot access. An insurance call alone cannot accomplish this.

Homes in areas like Black Forest and rural lots northeast of the city are particularly exposed. Pipes running through garages, exterior walls, and crawl spaces at this elevation freeze faster and fail more extensively than they would in Denver or Pueblo — often before discovery.

Spring snowmelt and summer storm basement flooding

El Paso County experiences significant basement flooding during spring snowmelt and after intense afternoon thunderstorms (May–August). When the water source is exterior floodwater rather than a supply line, it is classified as Category 3 under IICRC S500 — the highest contamination category, which requires removal of affected materials rather than drying in place. In these cases, your insurer also needs to know whether you carry separate flood insurance through the NFIP or a private carrier, since standard homeowners policies typically exclude exterior floodwater.

A restoration contractor identifies the water category on arrival — critical information for both the mitigation approach and the insurance coverage question. Your insurance agent can then confirm which policy applies once you understand what you're dealing with.

⚠️ Watch out: If the water source is a sewer backup or toilet overflow (Category 3 black water under IICRC S500), do not attempt to clean it yourself with consumer equipment. Sewage-contaminated water contains pathogens that require professional PPE, antimicrobial treatment, and proper disposal. Many Colorado Springs homeowners do not realize that sewer backup coverage is typically a separate endorsement — not included in standard homeowners policies. Check your policy or call your agent as soon as the contractor is on-site.

When should you call your insurance company immediately — alongside or before the contractor?

Call your insurer early or simultaneously in these situations:

  • If you're unsure whether the damage is covered at all. For example, slow leak damage (gradually developing over weeks or months) is frequently excluded from homeowners policies as a maintenance issue. Understanding your coverage before authorizing work is reasonable.
  • If the loss is catastrophic — significant structural failure, whole-floor flood, or damage affecting multiple units. Your insurer may want to assign an adjuster before major demolition begins.
  • If your policy requires prior authorization for work above a certain dollar threshold. This is uncommon in standard residential policies but appears in some commercial and landlord policies.

In practice, most Colorado Springs homeowners can call the contractor first and the insurer within the hour without any conflict. The restoration contractor and insurer operate from the same documentation and industry standards.

What should you have ready when you call both?

Have this information ready before calling:

  • Address and which part of the home is affected (basement, main floor, specific rooms)
  • Approximate time you discovered the damage
  • What you believe the source is, if known (burst pipe, appliance, backed-up drain, storm)
  • Whether the water source is still active or has been shut off
  • Your policy number and insurance company name (for the insurer call)
  • Photos or video you've already taken

This information speeds dispatch. Restoration contractors are accustomed to calls from homeowners who haven't diagnosed the problem — that's normal. You don't need to know what you're looking at to make the call.

Bottom Line

In almost every water damage situation, call a restoration contractor first and your insurance company second. The contractor stops damage from growing, documents the scene to IICRC S500 standards, and produces the moisture logs your adjuster needs. In Colorado Springs, where freeze-thaw pipe failures can run for hours undetected and spring basement flooding involves Category 3 water requiring immediate specialist response, the urgency is even higher. Your duty as a policyholder is to mitigate further damage — calling a qualified contractor fulfills that obligation. Frequently Asked Questions

Will calling a water damage contractor before my insurance company hurt my claim?

No — your homeowners policy requires you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and calling a restoration contractor is exactly that. Delaying mitigation while waiting for adjuster approval can actually give an insurer grounds to reduce a claim if damage worsened in the interim. Restoration contractors following IICRC S500 protocol document the pre-mitigation condition before touching anything.

How quickly does mold become a real risk after water damage?

The IICRC S520 standard recognizes that mold can begin growing in 24–48 hours on wet organic materials at typical indoor temperatures. In Colorado Springs homes, finished basements with drywall, wood framing, and carpet are particularly vulnerable — those materials are all organic substrates mold colonizes quickly when wet.

What if my water damage happened from a storm — does my homeowners insurance cover it?

Standard homeowners insurance typically covers sudden and accidental water damage from internal sources (burst pipes, appliance failures). Damage from exterior floodwater — including spring snowmelt entering a basement — is generally excluded and requires separate flood insurance through the NFIP or a private carrier. A restoration contractor can identify the water's origin and category (per IICRC S500) to help clarify which coverage applies.

What is a water damage contractor actually going to do when they arrive?

A qualified contractor following IICRC S500 will first identify the water source and category (clean water, gray water, or black water), then take baseline moisture readings with calibrated meters in affected walls, floors, and ceilings. From there, they deploy commercial extraction equipment and industrial dehumidifiers and air movers to begin structural drying — and log moisture readings daily as documentation for your insurance claim.

Does sewer backup work differently for insurance purposes?

Yes — sewer backup is typically not covered under a standard homeowners policy and requires a separate endorsement. Additionally, sewage-contaminated water is classified as Category 3 (black water) under the IICRC S500 standard, meaning affected porous materials must be removed rather than dried in place. If your claim involves a sewage backup, confirm your endorsement coverage with your insurer as soon as the contractor is on-site.

How do I know if I have a slow leak vs. sudden damage — and does it matter for my claim?

Slow leaks that developed gradually over weeks or months are frequently excluded from homeowners policies as a maintenance issue rather than a sudden and accidental loss. Signs of a slow leak include staining that looks old, soft or buckled flooring with no recent incident, and mold growth in areas adjacent to pipes. If you suspect long-term slow leak damage, confirm your coverage situation with your insurer before authorizing full remediation work.

How long does water damage restoration take in Colorado Springs?

For Category 1 (clean water) losses in a contained area, structural drying per IICRC S500 typically takes 3–5 days with proper industrial equipment. Category 2 or Category 3 losses, or larger areas, take longer. Colorado Springs's low humidity (typically 30–40% relative humidity) actually aids drying — a dry climate advantage that contractors account for when setting equipment and monitoring daily moisture logs.

What El Paso County regulations apply to water damage repairs?

El Paso County and the City of Colorado Springs require building permits for structural repairs that result from water damage — including drywall replacement and flooring replacement above certain square footage thresholds. Most homeowners aren't aware of this requirement. Restoration contractors familiar with the local market factor permit requirements into the scope of work and timeline, which matters for accurate claim documentation.
